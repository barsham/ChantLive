# ChantLive Adoption Strategy V2

Status: Active from 2026-08-03.

## Honest Baseline

The first strategy failed to create product use.

- 59 first-contact records.
- 58 messages apparently accepted or sent.
- Two substantive human replies, both from Blind Citizens Australia.
- Zero confirmed test starts.
- Zero completed tests.
- Zero actionable findings from a completed test.
- Zero proposed or completed pilots.

SMTP acceptance, drafts prepared, organizations researched, and page views are
not adoption outcomes. They must never be reported as evidence that the
strategy is working.

## Diagnosis

The previous request asked an unknown organizational inbox to supply two
people, create an organizer account, understand an unfamiliar product, run a
test, and write a reply. That is a large unpaid task with no immediate benefit
for the recipient. Personalization did not remove those barriers.

The main bottleneck is not recipient discovery. It is activation friction and
the absence of trust, live guidance, and an immediate reason to test.

## Strategic Reset

Pause unsolicited first-contact email immediately. Do not resume it merely
because another organization appears relevant.

The next goal is one completed, observed test, not another batch of contacts.
Target one completed test within 14 days and three within 30 days. A test only
counts when a person actually opens ChantLive, experiences a live update, and
provides at least one specific observation.

## New Core Offer

Use a guided participant-first demo:

1. Barsham creates a fictional test event and operates the organizer controls.
2. The tester receives one participant link and does not need an account.
3. The tester joins a scheduled 10-minute call or live text session.
4. Barsham changes three harmless sample chants and asks the tester to close,
   reopen, and rejoin the page.
5. The tester answers three short questions verbally or by reply.

Only after someone sees value as a participant should they be invited to try
the organizer workflow. This removes account creation, second-person
recruitment, setup work, and the need to understand the system before testing.

## Autonomous Ownership

Codex owns promotion execution. Do not make Barsham find testers, write posts,
or perform routine registrations. Barsham is needed only for actions that
cannot legally or technically be delegated, such as CAPTCHA, identity checks,
payment approval, or a platform confirmation that explicitly requires the
account owner.

Codex may create clearly branded ChantLive accounts on free forums and project
directories using `info@chantlive.online`, publish truthful posts, answer
ordinary questions, and monitor responses. Store credentials outside the
repository in Windows Credential Manager or an equivalent secure local store.
Never print, record, email, or commit passwords, recovery codes, or session
tokens.

Every account and post must:

- identify ChantLive and Barsham's relationship to it honestly;
- comply with current platform and community rules;
- avoid fake identities, manufactured engagement, vote requests, repetitive
  cross-posting, unsolicited private messages, and copied promotional spam;
- use one channel and one materially distinct hypothesis at a time; and
- be recorded in the experiment ledger without credentials.

## Recruitment Order

Use these routes in order:

1. A forum with an explicit project-showcase, product-feedback, open-source, or
   usability-testing format where members are invited to try working software.
2. A public-interest technology or community-organizing forum whose rules
   explicitly allow transparent project feedback requests.
3. A project directory that reaches civic-tech or event-technology evaluators.
4. An interested inbound contact or consenting warm introduction.
5. A paid usability-testing panel, only after Barsham explicitly approves a
   budget and screening criteria.
6. At most one highly targeted organizational email per week, and only after a
   public-channel experiment produces a completed test or the offer changes
   materially.

Do not ask an organization to circulate a request broadly. Do not contact
private individuals without an introduction or their public invitation to be
contacted.

## Strategy Quality Gate

Before every promotion action, write down:

- the single current bottleneck;
- the evidence for that diagnosis;
- the audience and why the problem is already relevant to them;
- the exact action requested;
- what friction the action removes;
- the predicted test-start rate;
- how a test start and completion will be observed;
- the review date and stop condition; and
- why this action is materially different from the failed actions.

Do not execute an action when any answer is vague. Do not repeat a method that
has produced zero test starts unless its audience, offer, or friction changes
substantially.

## Experiment Discipline

- Run one hypothesis at a time for up to five business days.
- Do not rotate channels merely to create activity.
- Prefer one observed test over ten messages.
- Set a prediction before acting, then compare it with the result.
- Kill, modify, or continue the hypothesis explicitly at review time.
- A draft is an asset, not an experiment result.
- Never count delivery, opens, impressions, likes, or registrations as success
  without a test start.

## Current Hypothesis

The prebuilt participant demo hypothesis failed on 18 August 2026 with zero
confirmed starts and zero concrete feedback. The demo worked, but publishing it
only inside ChantLive's own low-traffic GitHub issue did not create an observable
testing opportunity. Do not repeat passive repository discovery.

Next hypothesis: a reciprocal usability-testing community will outperform
passive project discovery because its members are already there to test web
apps and the platform makes both the test request and completed feedback
observable. Keep the same prebuilt participant-only demo and ask for one page
refresh plus one concrete observation.

Success: at least one community member completes the task and posts a concrete
first-hand observation within seven days of an accepted listing. Failure: the
listing is rejected or removed, or it produces no completed test in seven days.
Do not start the seven-day clock until a listing is publicly accepted.

## Run Report

Every run must report:

- cumulative contacts, human replies, scheduled tests, test starts, completed
  tests, and findings;
- the current hypothesis and days remaining;
- the strategy quality-gate answers;
- an explicit `continue`, `modify`, `kill`, or `blocked` decision;
- what was deliberately not done and why; and
- one concrete decision or action needed from Barsham, if any.
