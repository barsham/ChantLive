# SessionLab submission: Three-prompt clarity rehearsal

Status: prepared for submission on 19 September 2026. Not submitted or published.
Destination: SessionLab public methods library, not its discussion community.
Author: Barsham Sotoudeh, ChantLive maintainer.

## Name

Three-prompt clarity rehearsal

## Description

Rehearse three changing workshop instructions with a co-facilitator before a
session. Compare what the facilitator intends with what the reader understands,
then revise unclear wording and prepare a paper fallback.

## Goal

Leave with three clear instructions, an agreed signal for changing them, and a
fallback that a helper can use without needing a phone or a projector.

## Suggested settings

- Duration: 10-15 minutes, adjusted to the group's needs.
- Participants: 2-4 facilitators or volunteers rehearsing before an event.
- Difficulty: low.
- Suggested tags, subject to available library choices: skills, communication,
  workshop design, reflection.
- Materials: paper, pen, three draft prompts, and the display method you intend
  to use. A phone or laptop is optional. No specialist software is required.

These are planning suggestions. This newly drafted exercise has not yet been
evaluated with external facilitators; no outcome or accessibility guarantee is
claimed.

## Instructions

### Prepare three prompts: 2 minutes

Choose three ordinary stages of a forthcoming workshop: welcome, reflection and
close. Write the instruction for each stage in one short sentence. Put the same
three sentences on paper. Use fictional or non-sensitive wording for rehearsal.

Example prompts:

1. Welcome: Choose whether to listen, speak or pass.
2. Reflection: Think of one change that would make this workshop easier to follow.
3. Close: Choose one useful idea to take away.

These examples do not require the reader to disclose personal information.
Replace them with instructions appropriate to your session.

### Show, read and explain: 3 minutes

One person operates the display or holds up the paper prompt. Another takes the
reader's role. Show the first instruction without explaining it further. Ask
the reader: "What would you do next?" Record the words or missing information
that caused hesitation. Repeat for the other two prompts.

The reader may respond aloud, write an answer, point or pass. Ask which format
works for them. This rehearsal tests the wording, not the person's ability.

### Rejoin in the middle: 2 minutes

Ask the reader to look away while the operator changes the prompt. Then let
them look again and ask which instruction is current. If using a phone page,
close and reopen it once. Compare the visible instruction with the operator's
intended stage. Record any mismatch rather than assuming the page is current.

If you use paper, agree on a spoken stage name or clear pointer that identifies
the current row. Do not depend on colour alone to indicate the stage.

### Practise the fallback: 2 minutes

Put the device or projector aside. Give the reader the paper version. Have a
helper announce the current stage and point to its instruction. Check whether
the reader can follow without scanning a code or signing in anywhere.

The paper copy is static. If wording changes after printing, update it before
the actual session. This exercise is for routine workshop prompts, not emergency
instructions or safety-critical communications.

### Revise and hand over: 3 minutes

Rewrite one unclear instruction and repeat that step. Agree who changes the
display during the session, how the helper learns that it changed, and where
the paper copy will be kept. Note which parts still need checking with actual
participants; a rehearsal with colleagues is not a substitute for that feedback.

## Debrief

- What did the reader think they should do, compared with what was intended?
- Could they identify the current instruction after looking away or reopening?
- Did paper, a shared screen or individual phones work better for this situation?
- What one wording or handover change will you make before the workshop?
