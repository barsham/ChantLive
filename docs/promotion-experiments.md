# ChantLive Promotion Experiments

Use this ledger to test one unconventional promotion method at a time. The goal is qualified feedback and real-world pilots, not raw reach.

## Guardrails

- Contact only relevant organizations through public, official channels.
- Do not scrape or cold-email personal addresses.
- Do not publish to forums, directories, newsletters, or social networks without Barsham's review.
- Keep requests small: a 10-minute test, a short expert review, or an introduction to one suitable organizer.
- Stop contacting anyone who declines or opts out.
- Prefer a useful critique over an endorsement.

## Success Measures

Track these for every experiment:

- invitations sent;
- replies received;
- people who began a test;
- people who completed a test;
- actionable findings;
- pilot events proposed; and
- pilots completed.

## Experiment Menu

### Break ChantLive Challenge

Ask experienced organizers to spend 10 minutes trying to make a sample session fail. Prompt them to test weak mobile signal, accidental refreshes, late joining, confusing chants, screen locking, and organizer mistakes. Positioning the request as a failure-finding challenge can produce more concrete feedback than a general product review.

### Two-Device Test Drill

Invite a small group to a scheduled 15-minute remote drill. One person acts as organizer and another as participant. Capture where spoken instructions become necessary, then turn those moments into product findings.

### Accessibility Relay

Give accessibility communities a short testing matrix covering NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, keyboard-only use, zoom, high contrast, plain links, and QR alternatives. Ask each volunteer to test only one combination and pass the remaining combinations to others.

### University Lab Review

Approach Australian HCI, accessibility, civic-media, social-movement, and crisis-informatics research groups. Ask whether a student or researcher can use ChantLive as a compact usability-review exercise. Do not imply a formal research partnership.

### Organizer Office Hours

Offer one recurring 20-minute session where organizers can bring a real event scenario and test it with Barsham. Prepare the invitation and scheduling copy for review before publishing it publicly.

### Self-Hosting Pilot

Find one technically capable civic-tech or community group interested in evaluating deployment and data-control needs. Ask what documentation, threat model, and operational controls would be required before self-hosting.

### Newsletter Field Note

Pitch a short, non-promotional field note to relevant civic-tech, accessibility, open-source, organizing, or nonprofit-technology newsletters. Focus on the coordination problem, what has been learned from testers, and an invitation to challenge the design.

### Community Directory Listing

Identify curated civic-tech, public-interest technology, open-source-for-good, accessibility-tool, and community-organizing directories. Prepare accurate listing copy and submission requirements for Barsham to review.

### Scenario-Specific Test Kits

Prepare separate one-page test prompts for a rally, vigil, march, union meeting, faith gathering, and accessibility review. Each kit should use realistic sample chants and ask questions relevant to that setting.

### QR-Free Challenge

Ask testers to join and participate without scanning a QR code. Measure whether the ordinary link is easy to find, share, understand, and use with assistive technology. Use findings to avoid treating QR access as universal.
