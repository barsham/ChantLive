```text
Hello Pro Bono News editorial team,

I would like to pitch a practical 600-800 word field note for charities,
not-for-profits, advocacy groups, and volunteer-led organizations:

"Test community technology with two people before promoting it"

The article would offer a small, repeatable way to evaluate event and
coordination tools before asking a whole community to rely on them. One person
acts as an organizer, another as a participant, and they run a seven-minute
drill covering the ordinary joining link, late arrival, role clarity, a locked
or closed device, and three feedback questions.

The focus would be broader than any one product: reducing the burden on
volunteer testers, treating QR codes as optional, separating successful email
delivery from real participation, avoiding claims that have not been tested,
and measuring completed tests and actionable findings rather than reach.

I maintain ChantLive, a free and open-source event chant coordination tool,
which prompted this testing method. I would disclose that connection clearly
and mention the project only as a compact case study, not as an endorsement or
product promotion.

The piece would be original, nationally relevant, practical, and written as
best-practice advice. I can provide it as a document with a brief biography and
headshot if the idea is suitable.

Kind regards,
Barsham Sotoudeh
ChantLive
info@chantlive.online
```

## Proposed Article Structure

1. The adoption problem: community groups cannot afford a complicated pilot.
2. Why one-person demos hide organizer and participant confusion.
3. The seven-minute, two-person drill.
4. Test ordinary links, late joining, and recovery before optional QR codes.
5. Ask for three observations instead of general feedback.
6. Measure completed tests and findings, not impressions or messages sent.
7. Publish limitations and avoid untested safety, privacy, accessibility, or
   reliability claims.

## Submission Checklist

- articles should generally be 600-800 words;
- submissions may be comment, opinion, analysis, or best-practice advice;
- the topic should matter to a national social-economy audience;
- content must not be inherently self-serving or promotional;
- copy should be supplied as a document, not a PDF;
- URLs should appear in the copy rather than as footnotes or embedded links;
- a short author biography and headshot should be included;
- the article should be original, with Pro Bono News publishing it first; and
- the editor may edit for clarity and house style.
