## Useful public contribution

Source: Queer Calendar's open accessibility issue, "Venue accessibility

The issue explicitly asks the community to settle the accessibility facts and
wording before implementation. The repository contribution guide welcomes

issue was open; and used the existing authorised GitHub account.

At 6:05 pm Australia/Sydney, Promotion contributed a concrete data-model
safeguard: distinguish unknown, yes and no; preserve a source and verification
date; permit event-level overrides; retain a plain-language note; and avoid
turning absent feed data into a negative accessibility claim. The comment asks
whether the bilingual wording should be decided together with those states and
the verification rule. It discloses the maintainer context and AI assistance
and contains no ChantLive link, product offer or recruitment request.

Exact sent text:

> One modelling detail that may prevent misleading accessibility information:
> keep **unknown / not supplied**, **yes**, and **no** distinct. A missing value
> should not render as "no accessible toilet," and an old "yes" should not look
> permanently verified.
>
> A small first schema could be:
>
> - venue facts as tri-state values;
> - an optional source or contact for confirmation;
> - `last_verified_at` for facts that can change;
> - event-level overrides for temporary provisions such as interpretation, a
>   quiet room, or portable seating;
> - one plain-language note for details that do not fit the checklist.
>
> For feeds, I would include affirmative facts plus the verification date and a
> link to the event page, rather than flattening unknowns into negatives. That
> also gives organisers a clear update path without asking visitors to infer
> what an empty field means.
>
> Would it fit the community decision to define the bilingual wording together
> with the three states and verification rule, before the fields are
> implemented?
>
> Context: I maintain another open-source community-event tool. AI assisted
> with the wording; I reviewed the suggestion against this issue and the
> contribution guide.
