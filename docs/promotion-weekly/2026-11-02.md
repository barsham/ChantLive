Exact sent text:

> A practical evidence boundary from implementing a low-stakes community poll
> may be useful here: keep four things separate rather than treating the poll
> result as the outcome.
>
> 1. **Participant signal**: the responses received, plus an explicit statement
> that non-response remains unknown.
> 2. **Accountable decision**: the choice a named organiser or facilitator
> actually makes. It may differ from the largest count.
> 3. **Rationale**: why that decision was made, including constraints the poll
> could not capture.
> 4. **Observed consequence**: a later, concrete observation about what changed
> after the decision.
>
> That separation does not establish that the session helped participants, but
> it prevents response totals, apparent consensus, completion, or satisfaction
> from being promoted into an outcome claim. It also preserves a reason someone
> can answer for when the decision differs from the signal.
>
> A possible criterion candidate is: **Does the session record distinguish
> participant input, the accountable decision, and a later observed consequence,
> with each attributable and the limits of non-response stated?** Evidence could
> combine the session artifact with a bounded follow-up observation. The
> important limitation is that it still cannot recover experience that was
> never expressed, so I suspect this is an evidence precondition for the outcome
> layer rather than a sufficient outcome criterion by itself.
>
> I maintain ChantLive, an open-source community-event tool whose poll workflow
> prompted this distinction. AI assisted with the wording of this comment. I am
> not proposing the product as evidence.
>
> Would the stewards place this in the outcome layer, or treat it as a cross-layer
> evidence contract that outcome criteria depend on?
